1. Overview

CardioLens takes the 55 routine clinical measurements of one patient (demographics, examination, ECG, labs and echocardiography) and returns a calibrated probability of coronary artery disease (CAD) plus a calibrated probability of ≥50% stenosis in each of the LAD, LCX and RCA. Each probability is painted onto the matching artery of an interactive 3D heart. The dashboard beside the canvas explains every estimate with SHAP values, shows each measurement next to its contribution, and updates live as inputs change. A non-dismissible banner states that the tool is for decision support and education only.

2. Dataset and preprocessing

Data. Extension of Z-Alizadeh Sani (UCI id 411, DOI 10.24432/C5Q31M): 303 patients from one centre in Tehran, 55 clinical features and four binary targets. Positive rates: CAD (Cath) 71%, LAD 58%, LCX 39%, RCA 38%. There are no missing values or duplicates, and nothing was relabelled. Exertional CP is constant and was dropped.

Data audit and EDA (notebook Step 1)

  • Label logic: every one of the 216 CAD patients has ≥ 1 stenotic vessel (86 one-vessel, 67 two-vessel, 63 three-vessel). One "Normal" patient has a recorded stenotic vessel; it was kept as recorded, never relabelled. This structure motivated testing a hierarchical vessel strategy (§3).
  • Physiological sanity checks: 0 values outside plausible ranges for EF, K, Na, Hb, BP, pulse, BMI and creatinine; recomputed BMI matches exactly; neutrophils + lymphocytes never exceed 100%. Extreme but clinically possible labs (FBS 400, TG 1050, platelets 25) were kept.
  • Skewness: TG (3.8), platelets (2.6), FBS (2.3) and BUN (1.8) are strongly right-skewed, which is why linear and SVM models get a log1p transform. The EDA describes all 303 rows; the only choices taken from it (these transforms and the constant-column drop) do not use the labels.

Encoding (deterministic)

  • Y/N flags → 1/0; Sex → binary; BBB → one-hot.
  • VHD, Function Class and Region RWMA → ordinal.
  • Linear and SVM models also get log1p on skewed labs and standardisation, and drop weight, height and obesity (collinear with BMI).

Engineered features

  • Clinical scores: Diamond–Forrester pretest probability, eGFR (CKD-EPI 2021), chest-pain type, EF category.
  • Counts: risk factors, ECG abnormalities, ischaemic ECG, RWMA present.
  • Ratios: TG/HDL, LDL/HDL, NLR, PLR, absolute lymphocytes.
No leakage by construction. Every step above is a fitted scikit-learn transformer inside the model pipeline, so it is re-fitted inside each training fold and never sees validation or hold-out rows. The four label columns (Cath, LAD, LCX, RCA) are excluded from every model's inputs, and an assertion checks this before every fit.

3. Models and evaluation

Validation design

  • Hold-out: a stratified 20% test set (n = 61, seed 42), used exactly once at the end. Training set n = 242.
  • Model selection: repeated stratified 5×5 cross-validation on the training set, with nested Optuna tuning (inner 3-fold, 30 trials). All preprocessing runs inside the folds.
  • Thresholds: each target's decision threshold is the F1-optimal cut-off on pooled out-of-fold predictions: CAD 0.195, LAD 0.465, LCX 0.29, RCA 0.275.
  • Uncertainty: hold-out 95% CIs from 1,000 stratified bootstrap resamples.

Model architecture

Nine model families were compared for every target: logistic regression (ElasticNet), RBF-SVM, random forest, ExtraTrees, LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, and soft-vote and stacking ensembles of the top three diverse families. Three ways to model the vessels were also compared: A independent, B classifier chain, and C hierarchical P(CAD)·P(vessel | CAD). Strategy A was kept: it matched B (paired ΔAUC +0.002 ± 0.007) and beat C (−0.011 ± 0.019) while staying simpler. Every final model is calibrated with sigmoid (Platt) scaling, which gave the lowest expected calibration error (CAD ECE 0.033).

Feature selection (inside the folds only)

For each target we compared all 68 features against SHAP top-k subsets (k = 10, 15, 20, ranked by mean |SHAP| of a small LightGBM fitted on each fold's training part) and L1-logistic selection, and kept the smallest set within 0.01 AUC of the best. CAD and LAD keep all features (subsets lost 0.01–0.02 AUC); LCX uses SHAP top-15 (0.741 vs 0.747) and RCA SHAP top-10 (0.748, the best variant). No SMOTE was needed.

Reading the results honestly. CAD is predicted well (hold-out AUC 0.90) and LAD reasonably (0.83). LCX (0.77) and RCA (0.74) are only moderate: the dataset has no lesion location and just 303 patients, and the wide CIs reflect a 61-patient hold-out. The thresholds favour sensitivity (CAD recall 1.00 on hold-out) at the cost of specificity, which suits a triage aid but would need recalibration for screening populations with lower prevalence.

4. Clinical interpretability

Feature attribution. Exact TreeSHAP and LinearSHAP are computed for each ensemble member and propagated through the ensembling, the calibration and the vessel models. Results are expressed in probability points ("+7 pts") rather than log-odds, then mapped from engineered features back to the 55 raw clinical inputs and to six physiological groups (demographics, risk factors, symptoms, ECG, labs, echo). As a cross-check, LIME agrees with SHAP on 3.1 of the top-5 features on average. A full prediction with explanations for all four targets takes 149 ms (p95 150 ms) on CPU.

Clinical sanity and stability. The top drivers make medical sense: typical chest pain, ejection fraction, age, fasting blood sugar and hypertension for CAD; chest pain, regional wall-motion abnormality and EF for LAD; diabetes and age for RCA. Refitting on the outer folds of two CV repeats gives stable ranks (typical chest pain: mean rank 1.2 ± 0.4 for CAD and 1.1 ± 0.3 for LAD; age: 1.0 ± 0.0 for LCX). False negatives are mostly silent or atypical presentations (no typical chest pain, normal ECG, preserved EF), the same cases clinical pretest scores miss.

What the dashboard shows

  • Risk summary: CAD probability, risk tier (Low < 0.08 ≤ Moderate < 0.82 ≤ High, chosen so ≤ 5% of CAD patients land in Low), expected number of stenotic vessels, and a one-sentence plain-language explanation.
  • Artery cards: probability, threshold, "Stenosis likely" flag, a reliability badge derived from hold-out AUC (High / Moderate / Limited), and the top three drivers.
  • Why? panel: SHAP waterfall for the selected target and the group breakdown.
  • Measurement table: each value next to its typical adult reference range and its signed contribution, sortable by contribution.
  • What-if: change only modifiable factors (BP, LDL, HDL, TG, FBS, smoking, weight, DM, HTN, DLP). A toast reports the change ("Hypertension → No: CAD ↓21 pts"), labelled as a model-estimated association, not a treatment effect.
  • Model card: the metrics table with CIs, calibration, and the limitations below.

5. 3D pipeline and system architecture

Prediction → anatomy mapping

A pure function, scene_state(prediction), turns model output into a render-ready scene (≈0.3 ms, validated against a JSON schema). Prediction keys map one-to-one onto the meshes Coronary_LAD, Coronary_LCX and Coronary_RCA, which are the only risk-coloured structures. Every mesh name, colour and tooltip lives in a single anatomy_map.json.

  • Colour = probability, on one shared sequential scale for all vessels (#E8E2D8 → #F2B45A → #E4572E → #8E1B1B, interpolated in CIELAB, lightness L* 90 → 31). It reads correctly in greyscale and for colour-blind users, and equal colours mean equal risk.
  • Flag = decision. Being above a vessel's own threshold is shown by a separate non-colour cue: an outline, a pulse and a "Stenosis likely" badge.
  • No anatomical over-claiming. The dataset says only "≥ 50% stenosis somewhere in the vessel", so each artery is coloured uniformly along its whole length. The left main and side branches stay neutral ("not modelled"). Region RWMA is a model input, never a lesion map; a test enforces that no feature picks a vessel or a location.
  • Optional overlays: AHA 17-segment perfusion territories (right-dominant, Cerqueira 2002) and a body mode that shows where contributing factors live (lungs, kidneys, blood…), explicitly labelled "not the location of disease".

3D asset pipeline

The source is the Sketchfab model "Realistic Human Heart" by neshallads (CC BY 4.0). A headless Blender 5.2 script (python blender/make_all.py, about 5 minutes) re-aligns it to the anatomical long axis, scales it to a 12 cm base-to-apex length and splits it into parts. It then traces the painted coronaries on a cylindrical unwrap, using minimum-cost paths inside the anatomical grooves, and sweeps new continuous tubes 1.5–2 mm above the surface. It also generates the territory meshes, invisible 6 mm picking proxies, outline shells, camera anchors and a "Clinical" texture variant (KHR_materials_variants). Output is Draco-compressed with WebP textures.

Performance without a dedicated GPU. The viewer renders on demand (no frames while idle), caps the device pixel ratio (1.75, or 1.25 in lite), uses no real-time shadows, and switches to the 46k-triangle lite model automatically. The frame-rate figures above were measured on our development machine. Integrated graphics were not available to us, so that case is covered by these design choices rather than by a measurement.

Extensibility. A new clinical feature is added in cardio_ml/config.py and appears in the form automatically through /api/schema. A new model is registered in cardio_ml/models.py; the API and UI are model-agnostic. A new anatomical structure needs an entry in anatomy_map.json and a mesh with the same name in the GLB; the viewer binds by name.

6. Usage instructions

Install (one time)

  • Python 3.11: pip install -r requirements.txt then pip install -e .
  • Node.js 20 or newer.

Run

  • Windows, one click: double-click Start CardioLens.bat. It builds if needed, serves at http://localhost:8000 and restarts itself on a crash.
  • Any OS: python scripts/build.py then python -m app.backend.main.
  • Docker: docker build -t cardiolens . then docker run -p 8000:8000 cardiolens.
  • Development: python scripts/dev.py (API :8000 + Vite :5173).

Workflow

  • Click Sample patients and pick one (all are de-identified hold-out patients), or enter values in the left panel.
  • Rotate and zoom the heart; hover an artery for its probability; click it to open its card and explanation.
  • Keys: 1/2/3 views, B body mode, T territories, ? all shortcuts.
  • Change any input to see the result update; use What-if to explore modifiable factors.
  • Export a PDF report, a PNG or a text summary.

Reproduce and test

  • Retrain (seeded; writes artifacts/): place the UCI dataset in data/ and execute notebooks/track_a_ml.ipynb (CARDIO_FAST_MODE=1 for a quick run). Rebuild the 3D asset: python blender/make_all.py.
  • Tests: 49 pytest (ML, mapping, API), 22 Vitest (UI) and 34 Playwright end-to-end tests, including the colour-correspondence check.

7. Limitations and safety

  • Data: a single-centre referral cohort of 303 patients with 71% CAD prevalence and no external validation. Probabilities will not transfer to screening populations without recalibration.
  • Anatomy: labels are binary ≥ 50% stenosis per vessel with no lesion location, so CardioLens never shows where in an artery a lesion is. Territories are textbook averages.
  • Attributions are associations: SHAP and what-if describe the model, not causal effects. Some drivers are likely spurious at this sample size (e.g. height for LCX), and the model card says so.
  • Safety in the product: a persistent disclaimer, reliability badges on every estimate, the leakage guard in the API, a "Model uncertainty example" in the model card, and patient data kept in memory only (no storage, no analytics).
Disclaimer. CardioLens is for decision support and educational purposes only. It is not a substitute for formal diagnostic imaging or clinical judgement.
